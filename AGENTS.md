# Agent guide: donepudi.me

This repository is donepudi.me, Swarup's home page and, one label below it at hey.donepudi.me, everything behind its door: the static folder `site/`, served on both hosts by one Cloudflare Worker, `donepudi-me` (`wrangler.jsonc`, a route for each), which the `deploy` job in `.github/workflows/site.yaml` deploys on every merge to `main`. The two hosts are one rule, `site/hosts.js`: the threads' host is the home page's with `hey.` in front, derived from the host a request came to and never configured, so production, the local look and the tests follow it alike. The Worker splits every request by host first, then by path (`src/worker.ts`), and runs code only for the paths `run_worker_first` names. On the home page's host, donepudi.me, those are the home page, `/`, every update's page, `/updates`, its feed, `/feed.xml`, its videos' pictures, `/thumbs/*`, and `/api/talk`: the home page with the threads Swarup posted (each linked on the threads' host), his videos and releases and his Now line written in, and the same items as an Atom feed (`src/home.ts`), a video's picture the hourly run copied (`src/outside.ts`), and the note flow's send, which keeps a typed note as a new thread after a bot check and two daily limits. On the threads' host, hey.donepudi.me, they are the site's answer to Cloudflare Access's sign-in question for invited people under `/api/access/` (`src/evaluate.ts`), the threads' private calls under `/threads/api/`, behind their Cloudflare Access application (Swarup's journal's among them, under `/threads/api/journal`, `src/journal.ts`), a public thread's calls under `/api/threads/` with no sign-in, where anyone reads it, sends a note and answers its page's questions (`src/public.ts`), an ask's calls under `/api/asks/` with no sign-in, where anyone reads an ask and answers it into a thread of their own (`src/asks.ts`), and the threads page (`src/pages.ts`), which it hands over from `site/threads/index.html` through the assets binding at its root, `/`, and each room (`/threads/`, `/people` and a person's `/people/<n>`, `/meetings`, `/asks`, and the journal's `/journal`, a day or week at `/journal/<key>`, `/journal/storylines` and one storyline under it), at every thread's own address, because no file can sit at every number, and at every ask's (`/ask/<slug>`). Every path the code runs for on one host answers the code's JSON 404 on the other, so the threads' old addresses on the home page's host (`/threads/42`, `/ask/<slug>`, `/journal`) lead nowhere; nothing redirects from one host to the other. The rooms sit side by side at the top level; people lived at `/threads/people` before, and those addresses redirect to `/people` for good (the Worker's, in `src/pages.ts`). The threads' root, `/`, is Today, the door everyone signed in enters by (Today for Swarup, their threads for a person he invited) and where the sign-in lands when it names no page; `/threads/` is the Threads room, the list; `/asks` is Asks, every ask with its link; `/meetings/` redirects to `/meetings` and `/asks/` to `/asks` (`site/_redirects`), and `/journal/` to `/journal` (the Worker's, in `src/pages.ts`). Today's old address, `/me`, and the threads' older one, `/inbox`, lead nowhere on either host: the site's 404 page. It also runs every hour on a cron trigger (`triggers` in `wrangler.jsonc`), which fetches his YouTube episodes and the Planton and Speaking releases into the home page's list (`src/outside.ts`, its own table kept by `src/outside-store.ts`). Everything Swarup keeps is a numbered thread with a history, kept by one store (`src/store.ts`). Every other page comes straight from `site/`, on either host. Everything else it runs on (the zone, its records, the www redirect, the zone's settings, the database, the pages bucket, the bot check and Access) is declared under `infrastructure/` and applied by Planton; read [`infrastructure/AGENTS.md`](infrastructure/AGENTS.md) before touching any of it. These are the laws for any change here.

## Laws

- **Run `npm run check` before every push.** It runs the three strict type checks (the tests and `tools/`, the site's scripts and the page bridge, the Worker, that `worker-configuration.d.ts` matches `wrangler.jsonc`, and that `src/frame-bridge.ts` is what `tools/frame/frame.ts` makes of `frame/bridge.js`), wrangler's dry run, the site tests (`test/site.test.ts`, `test/markdown.test.ts`, `test/keys.test.ts`, `test/share.test.ts`, `test/people-words.test.ts`, `test/meetings-words.test.ts`, `test/asks-words.test.ts`, `test/journal-words.test.ts`, `test/standing-words.test.ts`, `test/page-talk.test.ts`, `test/bridge.test.ts`), the command line's tests (`test/inbox-cli.test.ts`, and the privacy probe's, `test/private-probe.test.ts`) and the Worker tests (`test/talk.test.ts`, `test/home.test.ts`, `test/outside.test.ts`, `test/threads.test.ts`, `test/threads-door.test.ts`, `test/members.test.ts`, `test/public.test.ts`, `test/asks.test.ts`, `test/journal.test.ts`, `test/people.test.ts`, `test/meetings.test.ts`, `test/evaluate.test.ts`, `test/pages.test.ts`, `test/routing.test.ts`, `test/migrations.test.ts`), which run the dry run's bundle in workerd, in front of `site/` with the routing `wrangler.jsonc` declares, and fire its hourly run through Miniflare's trigger path with every outside source played (`test/harness.ts`). Local commands run on the Node in `.nvmrc`. `npm run dev:signed-in` serves the same bundle with the home page at `http://localhost:8790/` and the threads at `http://hey.localhost:8790/` (a browser sends every `*.localhost` to the machine) with made-up threads and Access played as the owner (`test/dev-signed-in.ts`, with public threads, notes on one and visitors' answers on another's page, and made-up people of every kind; `--signed-out` for a visitor, who sees that thread's public view, `--member <address>` for a person invited to one thread; a made-up page that asks questions, with answers, discussions on its words and replies; meetings later today, next week, a day with no time and one past, with people who are invited and people with no address; an open ask answered from 34 browsers, some new and some read, one respondent invited back, a draft and a closed ask; a journal of three made-up days before today with a countdown, a photo, a hand edit, a typed answer and last week's chapter; the three public threads posted on the home page under a Now line, beside two made-up episodes, one with its picture, and a made-up release of each product), which is how the threads page and the home page are looked at in a browser before a deploy.
- **Code style.** Strict TypeScript with no `any`; the site's scripts and `frame/bridge.js` are plain JavaScript under `// @ts-check`. `tools/` is TypeScript that Node runs as is, so it uses only syntax Node can strip (no `enum`, `namespace` or parameter properties) and no dependency. Every file opens with a comment that says what it is for. `worker-configuration.d.ts` (`npm run types`) and `src/frame-bridge.ts` (`npm run frame`) are generated, never edited by hand.
- **The Worker has no runtime dependencies.** Everything it needs is in the runtime and its bindings, so the deploy installs nothing but wrangler. It answers only JSON, with `Cache-Control: no-store`, with six exceptions: a photo in Swarup's journal (`src/journal.ts`), the image itself with its stored type, `nosniff`, a sandboxing CSP and `Cache-Control: private, max-age=31536000, immutable`, because a photo is never overwritten; a page saved into a thread (`src/page.ts`), the stored HTML its frame loads, which is answered with its own sealing CSP and `Cache-Control: private, no-cache` revalidated by its ETag; and the threads page at a public thread's address (`src/pages.ts`), the site's own shell with that thread's title and link-preview tags written in by `HTMLRewriter` (as text or escaped attributes, never markup), fetched without the browser's conditional headers and answered without an ETag, so no browser keeps a stale title. Every other address gets the shell byte for byte. The home page, `/` (`src/home.ts`), is the site's own `site/index.html` with the threads he posted, the items from outside and his Now line written in the same way (each value as text or escaped, the items as markup that names classes only, never a `style` attribute the CSP would refuse), and each of its two sections shown only when it has something: with nothing posted, nothing from outside and no Now line, or when the read fails, it is the file byte for byte, its ETag kept. It shows the newest ten items, and "All updates" under them only when there are more; `/updates` is `site/updates.html` with every item written in the same way (up to 500, years at his pace), or its line saying there is nothing yet, and while the read fails it is that file with no list, as 503 with `no-store`. A video's picture, `/thumbs/<id>.jpg` (`src/outside.ts`), is the JPEG the hourly run copied into the pages bucket under `outside/`, answered for `GET` and `HEAD` with `image/jpeg`, `nosniff`, a sandboxing CSP and `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`, public because the home page is and cached for good because the page versions its address by the video's `updated`; anything else under `/thumbs/` answers the unknown path's JSON 404. Its feed, `/feed.xml`, is an Atom feed of the newest twenty of the same items, every value escaped as XML, with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=300` and `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`.
- **Nothing personal in a log line:** no name, email, address or token. A refusal logs its status and reason only. Cloudflare's automatic request records stay off (`invocation_logs: false` in `wrangler.jsonc`), because nothing says they leave out the visitor's address. No table keeps a visitor's network address either; the limits count a salted hash that lives one day. The only email addresses kept are a visitor's who typed one for a reply (a site note's sender's in `threads`, and a note's on a public thread in `comments`, each as typed, and lowercased in `people`, where it makes them a person Swarup deals with) and the ones Swarup gives people himself (`people`: an invitation's, which their sign-in needs, or one he adds to a person's page); a visitor's never reaches anyone but the owner and the sync.
- **Migrations are numbered and applied before the code.** `migrations/NNNN_name.sql`; the deploy applies them, then deploys. A migration adds, or drops only what no deployed code reads, in a deploy after the one that stopped reading it, so a failed deploy leaves a working site. It never renames. A table may be rebuilt in place in one migration, because a migration is one transaction and no deployed code ever sees it missing: copied aside, dropped, created anew under its own name with every column it had, its rows put back with their keys, the copy dropped, all under `PRAGMA defer_foreign_keys = on`, which is never turned off by hand (turning it off forgets the violations deferred so far, so the commit could no longer refuse a child left without a parent; `migrations/0012_people.sql` and its test). A new column arrives in the migration beside the code that first writes it.
- **The private door admits three callers: the owner's email, the sync's service token, and the email of a person invited to a thread, checked by the Worker on every request.** Access guards `/threads/api` at the edge, one application with three policies (the owner's address, the sync's token, then an external evaluation the site itself answers), and `src/access.ts` checks Access's signature, issuer, audience and times again on every call under `/threads/api/`, against that application's tag (`THREADS_ACCESS_AUD`), so a token signed for any other application never opens it, then names the owner's address (`INBOX_OWNER_EMAIL`), the sync's client id (`INBOX_SYNC_CLIENT_ID`) or any other address as a candidate, whom `src/threads.ts` admits only while that address has an invitation that stands (`isInvited` in `src/store.ts`); anyone else is refused with 403 `caller`. Access never guards a thread's own page, only its calls. A new caller is a new Access policy and a new check here, in one PR. The owner's page reads, starts threads of his own, makes the moves, comments and rewrites a thread's title, living summary or main text, every write only from his own page (`src/request.ts`). The sync reads, adds items (each once, by the origin it names), makes the page's moves, comments, rewrites a thread's title, living summary or the main text of a thread it wrote, sets a thread's meeting and its people, makes a thread an ask (a draft) and closes one, without the page's same-origin check, which guards only a signed-in browser; marking the owner's mail opened stays his, and filing an item as an issue is the sync's alone. A visitor's own words are never rewritten. The people calls (`src/people.ts`, under `/threads/api/people`) are read by the owner and the sync and changed by the owner alone, from his own page (a person's name, their address once while they have none, and his notes on them); an invited person is refused every one with 403 `owner-only` before anything is read, because a person's page holds what other people said to him. The threads page draws them for him alone (People at `/people`, a person at `/people/<n>`, their words in `site/app/people.js`); those addresses get the plain shell with nothing read (`src/pages.ts`), an invited person who opens one gets their own home, and a name on a thread opens its person only for him. Nothing it adds or moves happens without the owner's word in the sync; that rule is the sync's, and the Worker's part is to refuse what isn't one of these. Inviting a person to a thread, and taking it back, is the owner's alone, from his own page: the sync never invites anyone, because an invitation grants access. Being in a thread's meeting grants nothing (`attendees` in `src/store.ts`, never `members`): an attendee may have no address, one with an address is refused at the door and at sign-in like anyone uninvited, and that is why the sync may set a meeting's people (`test/members.test.ts` holds it). For the same reason, making a thread public and taking it back (`publish`, `unpublish`), putting it on the home page and taking it off (`post`, `unpost`), setting the home page's Now line (`/threads/api/now`, which the sync and an invited person are refused, read or write, with 403 `owner-only` before anything is read), opening an ask or marking its answers read, and dropping a visitor's note or answer on it, or showing it again, are his alone, and the sync never writes a comment for anyone (`audience` `public`), so nothing it transcribes is published without him.
- **An invitation opens exactly one thread.** An invited person reads the threads they are in, their pages, and comments on them (answers to a page's questions, comments on its words, and replies included), and nothing else: every read and their one write take the caller as the store's reader (`readable` in `src/store.ts`), so a thread they aren't in is a row the query can't see and answers exactly as a number never used, and every other call is refused with 403 `owner-only` before anything is read. What they see is shaped in `src/threads.ts`: never a visitor's email, an origin, an issue link, a drop reason, the owner's mail opening or an earlier version of any text, never a comment for the owner alone (`audience` `owner`, "Only me"), and the people by name only, a meeting's people included, their own place in it marked `you` by the address they signed in with (never by a name, and no address shown), so their page lists the meetings they are in (Your meetings). A test per route holds each of these (`test/members.test.ts`). The threads page is one app that knows its caller (`GET /threads/api/me`): an invited person gets their threads and a comment box, and none of the owner's controls, which the Worker would refuse anyway. The owner invites from his own page (Share, `site/app/share.js` holding its words and the draft), and the invitation is a draft his own Gmail opens: no mail is ever sent from the site.
- **A public thread is his own, and strangers read only what he chose.** Only a thread of his own (`source` `self`) may be public: a site note's and a told thread's words are someone else's. Every public query holds that condition in its own SQL (`PUBLIC` in `src/store.ts`), and every number that isn't a public thread answers the same 404 bytes on each public call (`src/public.ts`). A stranger reads the thread's title, summary, space, its main section as it now stands (a page's versions only while the page is its main section, never their claude.ai source) and the comments written as Public that are shown, by name: never its status, origin, sender, people, meeting, history, ids or last activity. A comment written before the thread went public keeps its audience. A visitor's note on a public thread runs every visitor note's checks (`src/note.ts`, shared with the note flow, and its daily budget) and is kept as a comment shown at once (`state` `shown`), which he may drop after the fact and show again (`dropped` and back); nothing waits for his OK any more (migrations/0015 showed every note that did). While the thread's main section is a page, a visitor may also answer the page's questions, with a name and no sign-in: each answer runs the same checks in the same order with its own daily budget (60 a day from one network and 300 in all, beside the notes' 20 and 60, `src/quota.ts`), and is kept as a comment for anyone on its question, shown at once, with the name typed and the SHA-256 of a key the visitor's browser keeps (`visitor_key`, the raw key never stored). The read takes that key from `X-Visitor-Key`, never a cookie: the newest answer per question and key is the one that shows (a dropped newest hides it, never bringing back an older one), and a stranger reads other people's answers to a question only once their own key has answered it, a rule the store's query holds (`historyPublic`). A visitor's words are always drawn as typed, never as Markdown, and invited people never read a dropped one or the events that would tell them of one; they see visitors' answers with the first 16 characters of the key's hash (`visitor`), so two visitors who typed the same name stay apart. A visitor's note is never an invited person's own, even under their address, because anyone can type one. A visitor's note or answer that is the last word makes the thread wait on him (`waiting`). Public threads are kept out of search results (`noindex` on the page, `X-Robots-Tag` on the calls), but anyone who tries a number reads a public thread: public means readable by anyone, not unlisted. Listing one is a second act of his: a public thread he posts (`post`, and `unpost` to take it off; `feed_at`, `migrations/0018_feed.sql`) is listed on the home page and in its feed, newest first, linked at its address, or its `?full` address when its main section is a page, by its title, its main text's first block as plain text (never the living summary), the day he posted it in his own zone and how many notes a stranger reads under it, and nothing he made public before is listed without him. The list also holds his full YouTube episodes (never a Short, which is cut from an episode) and the newest Planton and Speaking releases, fetched every hour with no act of his because they are already public where they come from, each with its title, its day and a link to where it lives, a video with its picture served from the site itself, so a visitor's browser asks nobody else for anything; a source that fails keeps its last good rows, and a video taken down or unlisted, or a release gone, leaves at the next run (`src/outside.ts`, `test/outside.test.ts`). A release gives way to his post about it: while a thread he posted names it as its origin (`release:<owner>/<repo>/<tag>`, the recorder sync's draft of a release post, which he rules on like any post), the home page and the feed leave that release's row out and tell the news once, as his story (`outsideItems` in `src/outside-store.ts`); a draft, a public thread he hasn't posted and one he takes off hide nothing. Only a public thread may be posted (409 `not-public`), the home page's query holds that in its own SQL, and taking a thread back from public takes it off the home page in the same statement, so making it public again never lists it again unasked. A stranger reading the thread never learns whether it is posted. A test per route holds each of these (`test/public.test.ts`, and the home page's in `test/home.test.ts`). The threads page draws a public thread for anyone who isn't in it (signed out, signed in to other threads, or removed from every one) in its public view, from the public call alone, with "Send Swarup a note on this" sent through the site's one sender (`site/talk-send.js`); when its page asks questions, a visitor types their name once (above the page, or in the page's bar in full screen) and answers in the boxes the bridge draws, as their browser's key (kept in `localStorage`, or for the visit when storage is off). A thread's address with `?full` opens its page in full screen at once; Share offers it as the link for answering. The owner makes a thread public from Share's first switch, and puts it on the home page from the second, under it, which is off until the thread is public (their words in `site/app/share.js`); Today's top row shows his Now line with Edit; a visitor's note is a comment with Drop and a Reply his own Gmail drafts, a visitor's answer has Drop on its line in the history, Undo shows either again, and a visitor's last word puts the thread in Waiting on you.
- **An ask is his own page, and each person who answers it gets a thread of their own.** An ask is one of his own threads (`source` `self`) whose main section is a questions page, with a slug (`ask_slug`, lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, at most 40, never changed once given) that names it at `/ask/<slug>`, and a state, `draft`, `open` or `closed` (`migrations/0016_asks.sql`). The owner and the sync make a draft from a page of his own that isn't public; only he opens it, from his own page, because opening puts it before strangers (the sync is refused with 403 `owner-only`); either closes it. An ask's thread is never public and never given a link, so its page stays its main section; its title is the one thing about it a stranger reads, so it is written for strangers, and its living summary, his private label, never reaches anyone through the ask's calls or its link preview, which carries the title and a fixed line, "A few questions from Swarup" (`src/pages.ts`). Anyone answers an open ask with no sign-in (`src/asks.ts`): each answer runs every visitor answer's checks in the same order (`src/note.ts`), with a name and an email both optional, because nobody but him reads an ask's answers; it counts against the asks' own daily budgets, apart from the notes' and the public pages' answers', so a link handed to a room of people never spends the note flow's allowance: the answer that makes a browser's thread first against `askThreads` (100 a day from one network, 500 in all), which caps how many threads strangers make, so a refused thread spends no answer, then every answer against `askAnswers` (400 and 2,000) (`src/quota.ts`). Each browser that answers gets one thread, by the SHA-256 of the key it keeps (the raw key never stored), kept in one batch with its first answer: a site note (`source` `talk`, so it is never public and its words are never rewritten), with the ask's title and space, an empty body, the ask's page as its main section and the ask it answers (`ask_thread_id`), found again only by its origin and that ask together (`ask:` is an origin no item the sync adds may take); its name and email are written when it is made, never after, and an email makes them a person, whose page shows their answers. Each insert holds the ask being open in its own SQL, so an answer racing a close is kept nowhere. A response thread is never given a page or a link of its own, because its page is its ask's, which everyone in it reads, the respondent he invites back included, though never the claude.ai address his page came from. That respondent reads their earlier answers as their own (`mine`, so their box holds the newest), by the address their thread was made with (`isAskAnswer` in `src/threads.ts`). A stranger reads an ask while it is open or closed (a closed one says so, with its page, and refuses answers with 409 `closed`), only its slug, title, state and page's versions, and only their own browser's answers, by the key in `X-Visitor-Key`, never anyone else's; a draft and a slug no thread has answer the same 404 bytes on every call. A response thread's first answer is a visitor's last word, so it lands in Waiting on you; Mark read, his alone from his own page, writes one `ask-read` event of his on each of an ask's response threads that waits on him now, so they stop waiting (never undone, because events are never deleted, never shown to invited people, and leaving each thread's last activity alone, so it can't be read as a receipt), and the ask's own thread lists who answered with whether each waits (`answeredBy`). A test per route holds each of these (`test/asks.test.ts`). A Send a note topic may name the ask it opens (`ask` in `site/talk-topics.js`), which the asks' list says. The threads page draws asks for him alone (Asks at `/asks`, its words in `site/app/asks.js`): drafts first, which only he opens, then every link with Copy link and Close, or Open again once closed; an ask's own thread with its state and link, and Answered by above its page, new answers first, eight until Show all, which lists everyone with a filter by name, and Mark read; a response thread with a byline that says who answered which ask, the ask's page with that person's answers under its questions and no box for him to type in; and on Today one row for several new answers to one ask, which opens it. Once read, a response thread leaves Today rather than showing as new. At `/ask/<slug>` the page draws the ask for anyone, signed in or not, from the public call alone, with no call to who is calling: its page full screen under one bar with a name and an email, both optional, until the first answer, then "Answering as"; a closed ask's page with no boxes and a way to send a note instead; and "Nothing here" for a draft or a slug no ask has. The email a visitor gives stays in the page for that visit; their name is kept with their key, as on a public page.
- **The journal is the owner's alone.** Swarup's journal (`src/journal.ts`, `src/journal-store.ts`, `migrations/0017_journal.sql`) is read by the owner and the sync, and by nobody else: it has no public call, and an invited person is refused every journal call with 403 `owner-only` before anything is read, as the people calls refuse them. An entry is a day's whole page text (`2026-11-06`) or a week's chapter (`2026-W44`) in the sync's page grammar, read by one reader (`site/app/journal.js`), stored byte for byte, at most 64,000 characters, 100 storylines and 100 photos; a save that fails for any other reason fails as itself, never as stale; a key that isn't a real day or ISO week answers exactly as a day never written. Every save names the version it was built on and is kept only while that is still the newest, in one batch that writes the version, its index row and its storyline lines together or not at all; a stale save writes nothing and answers 409 `stale` with the newest version, so the sync reads, rebuilds and writes again, and a hand edit is never overwritten. Versions are never updated or deleted. The owner saves (a hand edit) and types answers to a day's question only from his own page; the sync saves and uploads photos. A photo is a JPEG or PNG by its own first bytes, at most 4 MiB, kept in the pages bucket under `journal/` (the object first, then its row, the object deleted again when the row isn't kept), and a page shows only a photo already uploaded, named `photo:<id>`; any other image source is drawn as its caption alone. A week's margin repeats its days' storylines, so storylines are read from days only. No journal word is ever logged. A test per route holds each of these (`test/journal.test.ts`), and after every deploy the workflow's `private` job asks the live site for every journal address and the threads' private calls on the threads' host as a stranger, with no sign-in and with forged ones, and fails unless each is sent to Access's sign-in or refused, every journal page is the shell at the threads' root byte for byte, and the threads' old addresses on the home page's host answer 404 (`tools/probe/`, `node tools/probe/run.ts` by hand at any time). The threads page draws it for him alone: Journal on the rail and under More, every day week by week at `/journal` (a finished week's chapter opening its group), a day or a week at `/journal/<key>` with Edit for the whole text (his words kept per day while he goes elsewhere, until he saves or cancels; a save the sync overtook keeps them in the box with "The sync changed this day since you opened it. Copy your change, reload, and paste it in." and never merges), the storylines' trail and one storyline, and on Today the latest day's card with its countdowns, counted from today in his own zone, and its question with a box to answer it and Undo. Its addresses get the plain shell with nothing read, an invited person who opens one gets their own home, and signed out they get the sign-in door, which returns to that address.
- **Access's sign-in question has one answer, the store's.** The members' Access policy asks `POST /api/access/evaluate` at sign-in, and the site answers yes exactly when `isInvited` holds, the same question the door asks on every call, so the edge and the Worker can't disagree about who is invited. The answer is signed with the Worker secret `THREADS_EVALUATION_KEY`, whose public half the Worker derives and serves at `/api/access/keys`; without it both answer 503, which Access counts as no (`src/evaluate.ts`).
- **The owner's email address is in no file.** The repository is public. Access reads it from a Planton secret, the Worker from the GitHub environment `production`, and the threads page from his sign-in; the site test fails on any address in the repository's files.
- **The "Send a note" doors open the note flow.** The home page and the Talk to me page each carry one (`data-talk`, first among their doors), and `/#talk` and `/#talk/<topic>` open it too; the test holds both. The doors arrived only once the inbox (now the threads) was live and proven, so nobody writes into a box no one reads: a change that takes the threads away takes the doors with it.
- **One topic list.** `site/talk-topics.js` is the topics a note can be about, and the ask a topic opens when it names one, read by the page and imported by the Worker. It stays plain data and pure functions, with no page and no side effect; the Worker's type check (no browser library) and the site test hold that. `site/spaces.js`, what a thread can be about, follows the same rules and holds every topic; the site test holds that too. So does `site/app/markdown.js`, the threads' Markdown reader, which the Worker imports for a public thread's link preview, so Markdown is read one way on the site; and `site/app/journal.js`, the journal's reader, which the Worker imports to index each saved page, and which also reads no clock (today is always given, made by the page in the reader's zone); and `site/hosts.js`, the site's two hosts, which the Worker, the pages' scripts and the privacy probe import; and `site/app/standing.js`, where a thread stands: the lists the threads' calls answer (the Worker's `src/store.ts` and the command line's `tools/inbox/run.ts` import it, so a list means one thing everywhere), the page's two tabs, its words for each status and its orders, with no clock either.
- **`site/` is an allowlist.** Every file it holds is served, and the list in `test/site.test.ts` names each one. A new file is added there on purpose, never by accident.
- **Links are clean and root-absolute:** `/` and `/feedback`, never `feedback.html` or `index.html`. The Worker redirects those with a 307, and a relative link breaks on the 404 page, which is served at any path. A link from one host to the other (a post on the home page to its thread, the threads page's site name to the home page, Send a note's ask topics to their asks) is built from `site/hosts.js` and the page's own origin, never typed.
- **Never `no-referrer`,** on a page, an element or a header. The policy is `strict-origin-when-cross-origin` everywhere, so a site a reader leaves for learns they came from donepudi.me and never which page.
- **A page saved into a thread runs sealed, never on the site's own terms.** It is a Claude artifact's own HTML, so it is served only at its thread's call address with the headers `src/page.ts` sets (a CSP `sandbox` without `allow-same-origin`, claude.ai's page contract as its sources and no wider, `connect-src 'none'`), and framed by the threads page with the same sandbox tokens. The site's own CSP gains only `frame-src 'self'` for it; every library and font host lives in the frame's CSP alone. The one script the Worker adds is the bridge (`frame/bridge.js`, carried as text in the generated `src/frame-bridge.ts` because the frame runs only inline scripts and the bundle is one module), after a JSON script naming the site's origin; every page's ETag carries the bridge's hash, so a changed bridge never ships under an old tag. It speaks to the threads page by message alone and saves nothing: on its own it says the page's height and hides the page's `data-outside-thread` lines, and when the signed-in thread answers its `ready` it draws an answer box under each `data-thread-question` element in a closed shadow root, offers Comment beside selected words, and underlines the words each discussion is on with the CSS Highlight API, leaving the page's own markup alone. Every write is the threads page's, as whoever is signed in: words and replies are typed there, and an answer the page asks for is taken only while the frame holds the focus (the reader clicked or typed in it), at most ten a minute, for a question the page named, so a page script writing by itself at load is refused. The threads page tells the page only what the reader may read, by name, never an address or a person's number, because it can only post to `*`. A public thread's page that asks questions gets a visitor's `hello` (`visitor: true`), so it draws the answer boxes alone, for answers of at most 4,000 characters, and never Comment or underlines; one that asks nothing gets no `hello`, so strangers read it as it is. An open ask's page gets an ask visitor's `hello` (`mode: "private"`): the same boxes, whose note says only Swarup reads them, and never Comment or underlines; a closed ask's gets none, so its questions show with no boxes. The owner reading an ask's own thread or a response thread gets `hello` with `mode: "read"`: the answers the thread gives under each question, "No answer" where a page with answers has none, no box to type in, and Comment and underlines as usual. The four shapes are `helloFor`'s in `site/app/page-talk.js`, and the two a page always got are unchanged byte for byte. A visitor's answer is taken under the same gates as a signed-in one and needs their name first; a save that failed may say why in one short line (`saved`'s `problem`). A page is saved by the sync only, as a new version: the object in the private bucket first, then its row, the object deleted again when the row isn't kept; versions are never overwritten or deleted.
- **`site/_headers` matches the pages.** The test holds the expected set. A new kind of resource changes the CSP, and the test with it.
- **`wrangler.jsonc`'s `routes` is the Worker's whole set of addresses.** A route is declared there, never in Cloudflare's dashboard. Each deploy sends the list; depending on the key's rights, wrangler either replaces the Worker's routes with it or only adds to them and warns about the rest, so a route added by hand may survive a deploy unseen.
- **The Cloudflare key, the bot check's secret, the owner's email address and the evaluation key live only in the GitHub environment `production`,** which only `main` may use. The `check` job holds none of them. The `deploy` job installs nothing but the wrangler pinned in `deploy/`, refuses to run with any of the three Worker secrets empty, and hands them to wrangler in an owner-only file it deletes. The key can deploy Workers and their routes, read zones and edit the account's D1 databases, and nothing in DNS (`infrastructure/vendors/cloudflare/README.md`). Local runs use Cloudflare's public test keys (`npm run dev`), so no secret is ever on disk.
- **Never touch the zone's TXT records `_atproto` and `_dmarc`.** `_atproto` is what makes `donepudi.me` a Bluesky handle, and `_dmarc` is the mail policy. Their files under `infrastructure/` say so, and nothing in the site or the workflows can edit DNS.
- **Nothing private in any file:** no notes from outside this repository, no task or plan names, and no one's name but Swarup's.

## Design

The look is **Field notes**: an engineer's published notebook. Warm paper and near-black ink in light, warm charcoal in dark (from the device's setting), serif headings over a sans body, and one deep teal for links and actions. It is written for a working platform engineer and should still read as right in ten years: no trends, no decoration that doesn't carry meaning.

- **`site/style.css` is the only source of the look**, and its `:root` (with its dark twin) is the only place a value is written:
  - colour: `--bg`, `--surface`, `--ink`, `--muted`, `--faint`, `--line`, `--hover`, `--sel`, `--accent`, `--accent-ink`, `--accent-soft`, `--frame-bg`, `--shadow`, `--scrim`;
  - type: `--font`, `--serif` (headings and the journal), `--mono`, and the sizes `--text-3xs` … `--text-h1` plus the fluid `--text-sub`, `--text-lede-fluid`, `--text-title`, `--text-display`, `--text-hero`;
  - spacing: `--space-px` and `--space-1` … `--space-15` for every margin, padding and gap, plus the safe-area gutters.
- **Never write a literal colour, font size or spacing outside `:root`.** Borders, radii, widths and heights stay literal: they're shape, not rhythm.
- **Every text and background pair meets WCAG AA (4.5:1) in both themes.** The test computes it from the tokens.
- **Adding a token** is one line in `:root`, and one in the dark block when it changes with the theme. A token nothing reads fails the build.
- **No web font and no font host.** The CSP serves fonts from nowhere, and system faces cover every script.

`test/site.test.ts`, "the design system", enforces all of this beside the older rules (no unused class, no inline style, no third-party origin).
